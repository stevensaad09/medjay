Field Notes

A Taste of the Method

Short excerpts, one from each week of the MEDJAY programs. Read a few — if one lands, the full course is one click away.

Week 01

The first 10 seconds aren't for pitching. They're for safety. If a prospect's brain can't categorize you in that window, nothing you say after it gets heard.

Week 02

A "yes" feels like a trap. A "no" feels like protection. The openers that work give people permission to say no — that's what actually gets them to stay on the line.

Week 03

Stop asking "what's your budget?" Budgets are psychological thresholds, not numbers — and a direct question just makes people guard them harder.

Week 04

"I'm busy" isn't a rejection. It's a reflex. Treat it as a final answer, and you lose clients who were never actually saying no.

Week 05

A property doesn't sell because it's good. It sells because it matches how that specific buyer thinks — and that wiring is different for every person in the room.

Week 06

Every time you hand over information without context, you stop being an advisor and become a door-opener. Structure holds status. Speed gives it away.

Week 07

If a client leaves the room thinking exactly what they thought walking in, you added nothing. Respectful challenge earns trust — agreement doesn't.

Week 08

Headline numbers mean nothing. Net yield is what protects capital — and clarity, not hype, is what actually builds trust in the room.

Week 09

An objection is rarely a final answer. It's an uncalibrated clue. Defending your price the moment you hear one just confirms what the client already suspected.

Week 10

Artificial urgency breeds skepticism. Real urgency is always tied to a real consequence — and that's the only kind clients actually believe.

Week 11

Paperwork isn't the scary part. How you frame it is. Explain it with clinical simplicity, and the fear drops before it even starts.

Week 12

Your identity is your process. The market doesn't respect desperation. It respects structure, held consistently, under real pressure.
