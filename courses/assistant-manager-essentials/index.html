Assistant Manager Essentials

5 days · Self-study · Level 1 · Free
Download free Take the exam
Who It's For

For new and aspiring assistant managers, and for assistant managers already in the role. You will learn where your authority starts and ends, how to run the day, lead people, make routine decisions and build a record of reliability.

Outcomes

What you'll be able to do

  • Sort decisions into inside your authority, needs escalation and needs a record, and escalate early with a clear recommendation.
  • Sort your day's work by urgency and importance, write a short brief with outcome, steps, deadline and checkpoints, and agree a follow-up rhythm at handover.
  • Set an expectation in under a minute, run a fifteen-minute one-to-one, give feedback with SBI, and hold a boundary with A.R.T.
  • State a problem in one sentence, weigh two or three options by urgency and risk, make the routine decision and explain it briefly.
  • Hold your routines steady under pressure, and answer feedback you disagree with before you decide what to do with it.
The 5 Days

Day by day

  1. Day 1

    The Role and the Decision Map

    Day 1 defines the Three Roles, sorts decisions into Own, Escalate, Document, and covers agreeing boundaries with your manager and moving from peer to leader.

    • What you'll learn: how the Three Roles of Team Leader, Assistant Manager and Manager differ in a normal day, and the three choices for any decision: decide it yourself, escalate it, or record it.
    • How you'll think: escalating shows good judgment rather than failure.
    • Stretch goal: have a conversation with your manager that settles what you decide yourself, what you raise with them, and how quickly.
    • Frameworks introduced: Own, Escalate, Document; The Three Roles
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  2. Day 2

    Running the Day

    Day 2 covers urgent versus important work, daily planning blocks, opening and closing routines, written briefs, delegating with follow-up, and a short Daily Huddle.

    • What you'll learn: how urgent work differs from important work, planning blocks, opening and closing routines, a four-part written brief, and agreeing a follow-up pace when you hand work over.
    • How you'll think: planning, briefing and a short huddle are core parts of the role, not paperwork.
    • Stretch goal: hold a brief Daily Huddle where every item leaves with an owner and a deadline.
    • Frameworks introduced: Urgency and Importance; Short Daily Team Huddle
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  3. Day 3

    Leading People

    Day 3 covers setting expectations that stick, one-to-one check-ins, SBI feedback on a missed commitment, holding a boundary with A.R.T., and recognition.

    • What you'll learn: what makes up a clear expectation, what a brief one-to-one is for, and the three steps each of SBI and A.R.T.
    • How you'll think: assume a missed commitment is usually an expectation problem before it is a people problem, and see a firm boundary as fair to the whole team.
    • Stretch goal: hold the line if the person argues twice, without hardening your tone or relaxing the standard.
    • Frameworks introduced: SBI: Situation, Behavior, Impact; A.R.T.: Acknowledge, Reframe, Transition (MEDJAY's own framework, developed by Steven Saad)
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  4. Day 4

    Problems and Decisions

    Day 4 covers separating facts from assumptions, weighing options by urgency and risk, escalating with a recommendation, and acting as manager when yours is away.

    • What you'll learn: why problems grow when facts and guesses blur together, and how to pull them apart.
    • How you'll think: escalation with a recommendation is a strength, and guessing is the real risk.
    • Stretch goal: step in as manager when yours is away, including calmly handling a customer escalation within your authority.
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  5. Day 5

    Reliability and Growth

    Day 5 covers a single commitments list, the Weekly Update, routines that hold under pressure, and the promotion conversation.

    • What you'll learn: how to keep one list of commitments, send your manager a short Weekly Update, keep records that someone else could take over, and apply A.R.T. when feedback comes in that you disagree with.
    • How you'll think: reliability is a visible, measurable behavior, and escalation is a strength.
    • Stretch goal: argue for a promotion using your record, not adjectives, then set out a three-phase plan for your first 90 days.
    • Frameworks introduced: Weekly Update
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list

How It Works

How each day works

Each day is a self-study guide you work through alone, applying every idea to your actual team and task list and writing as you go. A version of each practice task is provided for people who do not hold the role yet.

Exam

Exam and certificate

  • The Level 1 exam has 20 multiple-choice questions, one correct answer each. Pass with 14 or more. There is no time limit, and you can retake it as often as you like.
  • After you pass, you enter your full name and email, check how your name will appear, and confirm. One certificate is kept per course and email, and its link is sent by email.
  • Each certificate has a unique number in the form MJ-AME-year-code and reads "Certificate of Completion" for "Assistant Manager Essentials — Level 1", signed by Steven Saad, Founder.
  • The certificate page has a "Download PDF" button (it opens the print dialog, and the page says to choose "Save as PDF" as the printer), an "Add to LinkedIn profile" link and a "Verify" link.
  • The verification page shows Status, Full name, Course, Issue date and Certificate No, and never your email.

Questions

FAQ

Is this course free?

Yes. It is a free Level 1 self-study course, and passing the exam lets you claim a free certificate.

How does the exam work?

The exam has 20 multiple-choice questions, and you pass with 14 or more. There is no time limit, and you can retake it as often as you like.

Do I need to be an Assistant Manager to take it?

No. You may already hold the title or still be a Team Leader or senior team member working toward it, and every practice task has an option for someone who is not an Assistant Manager yet.

What format is the course?

Each day is a PDF study guide, five in total. On the download page you can take them one at a time, or all five together as one zip file.
