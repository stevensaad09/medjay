Team Leader Essentials

5 days · Self-study · Level 1 · Free
Download free Take the exam
Who It's For

For new and aspiring team leaders, and for team leaders already in the role who want a clear method. You will learn what changes when you move from top performer to leader, and how to set expectations, coach, handle underperformance and build trust.

Outcomes

What you'll be able to do

  • Delegate a task with a clear briefing, and set standards that are specific, equal for everyone and checkable.
  • Write a clear expectation with a measure of success, run a 10-minute huddle, and give feedback that is specific, factual and easy to act on.
  • Run a short coaching conversation using the Question Ladder, and give feedback on behavior and its impact rather than on character.
  • Run a structured underperformance conversation, and mediate a clash between two team members using A.R.T.
  • Design a recognition approach for each team member, and connect their work to something that matters to them.
The 5 Days

Day by day

  1. Day 1

    From Top Performer to Leader: What Changes

    Day 1 covers the shift from doing the work to leading it, earning credibility, setting standards that stick, and managing your time in three directions.

    • What you'll learn: what changes when you go from doing the work to leading others, and what research reports about how new leaders handle that shift.
    • How you'll think: accept that you are now judged on the team's results rather than your own.
    • Stretch goal: this week, talk expectations with a team member, ideally the one you would rather avoid.
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  2. Day 2

    Setting Expectations and Running a Daily Huddle

    Day 2 covers setting expectations that mean something, building trust with clear measures, running a Daily Huddle, and giving feedback with SBI.

    • What you'll learn: SBI feedback and the five elements of a focused Daily Huddle.
    • How you'll think: see unclear expectations, rather than lazy people, as the usual cause of drifting performance.
    • Stretch goal: convert a blocker that keeps surfacing in huddles into a lasting change in how the team works.
    • Frameworks introduced: SBI: Situation, Behavior, Impact; Daily Huddle (five parts)
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  3. Day 3

    Coaching and Feedback That Changes Behavior

    Day 3 moves you from telling to coaching, with the Question Ladder, feedback on behavior rather than personality, and a coaching conversation after a missed deadline.

    • What you'll learn: the four rungs of the Question Ladder, and why leaders skip the Impact rung most often.
    • How you'll think: be the one asking questions rather than giving answers, since a solution people reach themselves lasts longer.
    • Stretch goal: record a short coaching conversation, review it against the ladder, and spot where you slipped into telling.
    • Frameworks introduced: Question Ladder: Situation, Problem, Impact, Need
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  4. Day 4

    Handling Underperformance and Conflict

    Day 4 covers what underperformance really is, a structured conversation about it, conflict between team members, and an angry customer.

    • What you'll learn: why dealing with conflict early costs less, why people distance themselves from the problem, and how A.R.T. opens a hard conversation.
    • How you'll think: raising a problem serves the team; your silence is a decision.
    • Stretch goal: carry one real underperformance or conflict issue from the initial private conversation to the follow-up, and record what was agreed.
    • Frameworks introduced: A.R.T.: Acknowledge, Reframe, Transition (MEDJAY's own framework, developed by Steven Saad); Five-Step Conversation for Underperformance
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list
  5. Day 5

    Motivating, Recognizing, and Building Trust

    Day 5 covers what goals help and hurt, what motivates different people, recognition that feels real, and trust.

    • What you'll learn: the research on specific goals, both benefits and risks, and why people are motivated by different things.
    • How you'll think: recognition is planned leadership behavior, and trust comes mainly from consistency and fairness.
    • Stretch goal: write a personal leadership development goal using the four KASU parts.
    • Frameworks introduced: KASU (Knowledge, Attitude, Skill, Upskill) as a goal-setting tool
    • What's inside: "What Would You Say?" exercises, Apply It in Your Role, a quiz, an Upskill Challenge and a reference list

How It Works

How each day works

Each day is a self-study guide you work through alone, writing your answers first and then comparing them with the model answers at the end. Each section runs explanation, example, reflection. You keep one notebook or document for the whole course.

Exam

Exam and certificate

  • The Level 1 exam has 20 multiple-choice questions, one correct answer each. Pass with 14 or more. There is no time limit, and you can retake it as often as you like.
  • After you pass, you enter your full name and email, check how your name will appear, and confirm. One certificate is kept per course and email, and its link is sent by email.
  • Each certificate has a unique number in the form MJ-TLE-year-code and reads "Certificate of Completion" for "Team Leader Essentials — Level 1", signed by Steven Saad, Founder.
  • The certificate page has a "Download PDF" button (it opens the print dialog, and the page says to choose "Save as PDF" as the printer), an "Add to LinkedIn profile" link and a "Verify" link.
  • The verification page shows Status, Full name, Course, Issue date and Certificate No, and never your email.

Questions

FAQ

Is this course free?

Yes. It is a free Level 1 self-study course, and passing the exam lets you claim a free certificate.

How does the exam work?

The exam has 20 multiple-choice questions, and you pass with 14 or more. There is no time limit, and you can retake it as often as you like.

What format is the course?

Each day is a PDF study guide, five in total. On the download page you can take them one at a time, or all five together as one zip file.

Do I have to give my email to download?

No. The download page links each day's PDF and the zip file directly, and its email form is labeled "Stay in touch (optional)".
