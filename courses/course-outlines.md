# Track: Frontline Sales

## Retail Sales Essentials
Slug: retail-sales-essentials

### Day 1: How Customers Decide

- Knowledge: You will know the difference between fast, automatic thinking and slow, deliberate thinking, and why first impressions are formed in the fast mode before a customer evaluates anything.
- Attitude: You will start seeing your job as helping a person feel safe enough to decide, rather than convincing them with more information.
- Skill: You will be able to recognize buying signals, match your approach to a customer's decision style, and adapt when a customer resists.
- Upskill: You will practice one real interaction from start to finish, observe which mode of thinking the customer was in, and note what you would do differently next time.

### Day 2: Opening Conversations and Discovering Needs

- Knowledge: You will know the 4 steps of the First-Contact Standard and the 4 rungs of the Question Ladder, and why each one works on the customer's mind.
- Attitude: You will shift from "I need to present something" to "I need to understand something." A good opening earns the right to keep talking. It doesn't demand it.
- Skill: You will be able to open a conversation with a walk-in and climb the Question Ladder from situation to need, using open questions and listening more than you speak.
- Upskill: You will run an opening and discovery conversation with a real customer, supervised, and review it afterward against the First-Contact Standard and the Question Ladder.

### Day 3: Presenting Value and Handling Objections

- Knowledge: You will know the three steps of A.R.T., MEDJAY's framework for handling objections and concerns, developed by Steven Saad, and why each step works on the customer's side of the conversation.
- Attitude: You will see an objection as information about what the customer still needs to believe, not as a rejection of you or the product.
- Skill: You will be able to present one product in Feature-Advantage-Benefit (FAB) form tied to a need the customer stated, and to run Acknowledge, Reframe and Transition together, in order, on a real objection.
- Upskill: You will be able to stay steady and unhurried when the same objection comes back a second time, without repeating your answer louder or faster.

### Day 4: The Retail Floor: Approaching Walk-Ins and Add-On Selling

- Knowledge: You will know the A.I.M. offer structure (Anchor, Illuminate, Mobilize) and why customers give or refuse add-on suggestions.
- Attitude: You will treat a walk-in's first seconds as their decision to make, not yours to force.
- Skill: You will be able to read openness signals, time your approach, and make one add-on suggestion using A.I.M.
- Upskill: You will be able to recover a customer who steps back or says "just looking" without pressure, and still earn a second conversation.

### Day 5: Closing at the Counter, After-Sale, and Building Repeat Customers

- Knowledge: You will know the 5 steps of the Commitment Close and the 5 conditions to check before you ask for any commitment.
- Attitude: You will treat the close as a service to the customer rather than a favor you are asking for, and treat after-sale care as part of the sale, not an extra.
- Skill: You will be able to close a counter sale using a calibrated how or what question, or a genuine choice with a date, and practice it whether or not you have a retail job yet.
- Upskill: You will be able to handle one after-sale problem calmly and turn it into a reason for the customer to come back.

## Counter Sales Essentials
Slug: counter-sales-essentials

### Day 1: How Counter Sales Works

- Knowledge: You will know the main types of counters, the two kinds of customer they serve, the five measures used in counter sales, and the sources you use to find product information.
- Attitude: You will see that counter selling is service and accuracy, not pushiness. The customer came to you, so your job is to find the job behind the request and get it right the first time.
- Skill: You will be able to walk through a counter sale in the correct order, from greeting to receipt, and explain what happens at each step.
- Upskill: You will be able to look up a product you do not know and give the customer a confident, accurate answer, or tell them honestly when you need to check.

### Day 2: Opening and Finding the Real Need

- Knowledge: You will know the four behaviors of the First-Contact Standard, in order, and why each one lowers a customer's guard.
- Attitude: You will treat the opening minute as a skill to practice deliberately, not as small talk that happens by accident.
- Skill: You will be able to open a counter sale with a customer and a queue waiting, and ask questions that uncover the job behind the request.
- Upskill: You will be able to open, acknowledge the queue and ask your first need-finding question inside one calm, unhurried minute, then review your own recording for what to keep and what to drop.

### Day 3: Recommending and Adding What the Job Needs

- Knowledge: You will know how to present one or two clear options in the customer's own terms, how good-better-best helps only when it fits the job, what makes an add-on honest, and how to answer price and out-of-stock questions without overpromising.
- Attitude: You will shift from showing everything you know to giving the customer the right thing the first time, and you will treat an unneeded add-on as a cost to your next visit, not a win.
- Skill: You will be able to build a two-option recommendation for a real or fictional counter customer, phrase an add-on as a question about the job, offer a clear substitute or order time, and answer a price question in one calm sentence.
- Upskill: You will be able to handle an out-of-stock item and a price question in one calm exchange: a real substitute or an order with a day you can keep, then a plain price followed by a pause.
- The First-Contact Standard you learned on Day 2 still opens every counter conversation. Today picks up after it: the moment you know what the customer is trying to do.

### Day 4: Objections and Hard Moments at the Counter

- Knowledge: You will know why customers push back at the counter, and the three steps of A.R.T., MEDJAY's own framework: Acknowledge, Reframe, Transition.
- Attitude: You will see a price objection as information about fear, not as an attack on your product or on you.
- Skill: You will be able to handle a price objection using A.R.T. as one chain, with the Mandatory Pause before you answer.
- Upskill: You will be able to answer a discount request you cannot grant with a calm, polite no, and keep the customer with you.

### Day 5: Closing, Getting the Order Right, and Your First 90 Days

- Knowledge: You will know the five steps of the Commitment Close and the checks that make an order, payment and receipt correct.
- Attitude: You will see closing as the logical end of a good conversation, not as pressure, and accept that the customer can always say no.
- Skill: You will be able to close a counter sale with a genuine choice, confirm an order item by item, and turn a first sale into a regular customer with honest follow-up.
- Upskill: You will be able to show these skills out loud in a hiring role-play or interview, and plan how to learn on the job in your first 90 days.

## Tele Sales Essentials
Slug: tele-sales-essentials

### Day 1: How Tele Sales Works and How You Prepare a Session

- Knowledge: You will know the difference between outbound and inbound tele sales, between B2C and B2B selling, and what each common performance metric is actually telling you about buyer behavior.
- Attitude: You will treat preparation and calm as professional skills, not extras. A prepared rep who respects the person on the phone is a professional, not a script reader.
- Skill: You will be able to build a short calling list with one true, recognizable reason per contact, and settle your nerves before a session with a slow-exhale breathing routine.
- Upskill: You will record yourself reading a first contact aloud and score your own pace, clarity and calm against a simple checklist.

### Day 2: Opening the Call and Reading the First Seconds

- Knowledge: You will know the four steps of the First-Contact Standard and how to adapt them for an inbound sales call.
- Attitude: You will see the first seconds of a call as something you earn, not something you are owed. Calm and interest beat fast and loud.
- Skill: You will be able to open an outbound call and answer an inbound one with a short, clear opening, a pace that sounds credible, and one easy question at the end.
- Upskill: You will be able to ask a gatekeeper for help finding the right person, and to tell a brush-off from a real no inside the first minute.

### Day 3: Finding the Real Need by Phone

- Knowledge: You will know why short open questions, one at a time, find more than a list of questions fired quickly, and why too many options stall a decision.
- Attitude: You will shift from pitching to understanding. Your job in discovery is to help the client describe their situation in their own words.
- Skill: You will be able to run a short discovery conversation by phone, reflect back two things you heard, and offer one or two options instead of five.
- Upskill: You will build a personal question bank of five short open questions and test it on three practice calls, noting which question produced the most useful answer.

### Day 4: Objections, the Pause and A.R.T.

- Knowledge: You will know why the Mandatory Pause before an answer calms a call, and the three steps of A.R.T., MEDJAY's own framework for objections: Acknowledge, Reframe, Transition.
- Attitude: You will treat an objection as information and a sign of interest, not as an attack, and you will stop rushing to defend.
- Skill: You will be able to answer the four most common phone objections using the Mandatory Pause and the full A.R.T. chain, ending each one with an open how or what question.
- Upskill: You will be able to take three objections in a row without losing your pace or your calm, and to recognize and respect a firm no on the spot.

### Day 5: Closing, Following Up and the Hiring Role-Play

- Knowledge: You will know the five steps of the Commitment Close and why a client who can still say no commits more firmly.
- Attitude: You will treat a close as the logical conclusion of a good conversation, not as pressure you apply at the end.
- Skill: You will be able to close a call with a genuine choice and a date, confirm the agreement in the customer's own words, and log the call.
- Upskill: You will be able to run a full recorded mock call from opener to close and review it against a self-check list, as practice for a hiring role-play.

## B2B Sales Essentials
Slug: b2b-sales-essentials

### Day 1: Know the Client: Research, Target Accounts, and a Reason to Reach Out

- Knowledge: How to research a B2B client's business model, industry pressures and likely priorities, and how to use that research to choose target accounts and build a reason to reach out.
- Attitude: Research first, contact second. A relevant reason to reach out is earned by understanding the client's business, not by a clever phrase.
- Skill: You can build a short target account list and write one specific, checkable reason to reach out to each business on it.
- Upskill: You can test your research hypothesis against a real client conversation, note where your reading of their priorities was wrong, and correct it for the next contact.

### Day 2: Reaching the Right Person: Outreach and the First Conversation

- Knowledge: Understand why a business contact decides in the first seconds whether to keep listening, and how pressure, tone and relevance shape that decision.
- Attitude: Treat early brush-offs as reflexes, not verdicts, and stay calm and useful whether the person says yes or no.
- Skill: Find the right contact in a target business, send one relevant outreach message, and open a first conversation that ends with a specific next step.
- Upskill: Handle a repeated brush-off or a clear refusal with a respectful exit, and log the outcome accurately, including any request not to be contacted again.

### Day 3: Discovery in B2B: Business Problems and the Cost of Doing Nothing

- Knowledge: You will know the four-rung question ladder (situation, problem, impact, need) and why the impact rung matters most.
- Attitude: You will treat discovery as listening with a purpose, not as a polite pause before your pitch.
- Skill: You will ask open how and what questions that surface a client's business problem and what it costs them to leave it unsolved.
- Upskill: You will use at least three of these questions in real conversations and record what changed in your next step because of what you heard.

### Day 4: Several Decision Makers: Stakeholders, Objections, and Price Conversations

- Knowledge: You will know how a B2B decision-making unit is structured, the four friction blocks behind most objections, and why "the price is too high" is rarely a final statement about money.
- Attitude: You will treat every stakeholder as a person with their own risk and their own reason to say no, and treat price objections as an invitation to understand value, not as an attack to survive.
- Skill: You will be able to map the stakeholders in a deal, diagnose an objection from the client's own words, and hold a price conversation that connects the number to an outcome.
- Upskill: You will be able to run a price conversation with more than one stakeholder in the room, keep your composure when two of them push in different directions, and leave with one agreed next step.

### Day 5: Moving the Deal: Next Steps, Follow-Up, and Closing

- Knowledge: Understand what a buying signal is, why a clear next step protects the client's decision, and why a calibrated question moves a deal further than a yes-or-no close.
- Attitude: Shift from "getting the signature" to "structuring the client's thinking so their decision is their own." Accept that a well-run close can end in "not yet" and still be a success.
- Skill: Close every conversation with a specific next step, including a date, and use the Commitment Close to ask for commitment without pressure.
- Upskill: Run a full close from start to finish in a role-play, then apply it in one real, supervised client conversation, and write down what held you back.

# Track: Customer Service

## Customer Service Essentials
Slug: customer-service-essentials

### Day 1: What the Job Is and What the Customer Has Already Lost

- Knowledge: You will know what a customer service representative does across phone, chat, email and the service counter, what a case is, and what the common team measures mean in plain words.
- Attitude: You will see the customer as someone who has already paid a cost before contacting you, not as an interruption or a complaint to be cleared.
- Skill: You will be able to explain a felt loss in a customer's own words and identify fast, automatic reactions versus slow, deliberate thought in a real service conversation.
- Upskill: You will record and review one real support interaction you took part in as a customer, noting the losses the customer carried in and every deposit or withdrawal you observed.

### Day 2: First Contact and Finding the Real Problem

- Knowledge: You will know the four steps of the First-Contact Standard (Low Threat, Autonomy, Relevance, Curiosity) and the four rungs of the Question Ladder (Situation, Problem, Impact, Need).
- Attitude: You will treat the first contact as something you design, not something that happens to you, and you will accept that the customer's first sentence is rarely the whole problem.
- Skill: You will be able to open a contact the customer chooses to continue, repeat their reason back in their own words, split the event from the impact, and ask how and what questions instead of why.
- Upskill: You will run one full practice contact using all four steps and all four rungs, in order, and write one follow-up that uses a how or what question.

### Day 3: Angry Customers and Complaints: Staying Steady and Moving Forward

- Knowledge: You will know why naming a customer's feeling out loud lowers the heat, and what A.R.T. steps you through when a complaint reaches you.
- Attitude: You will treat anger as a signal of loss rather than a personal attack, so you stop defending and start resolving.
- Skill: You will run A.R.T. on a complaint in a live role-play, and you will keep your voice calm and factual while a customer raises their voice.
- Upskill: You will complete the 10-Round Interruption Drill and score yourself honestly, including on the rounds where a customer interrupts, where you run the full A.R.T. chain, where you hand over to a supervisor, and where the customer ends the contact before it is resolved.
- Write one sentence now: a time you were the angry customer, and what you most wanted the person on the other end to do.
- | **NOTES** You will return to that sentence at the end. Do not skip it. |
- | --- |

### Day 4: Phone, Chat and Email: Messages That Hold Up

- Knowledge: You will know the three parts of a message that holds up (what you know, what you are doing, when they will hear from you) and the four dials (detail, warmth, speed, words).
- Attitude: You will treat each message as something the customer may reread, quote or act on alone, not as words that vanish once spoken.
- Skill: You will be able to write a chat message, an email and a case note that another person could act on without calling the customer back.
- Upskill: You will be able to adjust the four dials between a frustrated customer and a relaxed one in the same channel, and explain why you turned each dial where you did.

### Day 5: Closing, Keeping Promises and What Recovery Can Do

- Knowledge: You will know the closing sequence for a simple ending, the 5 steps of the Commitment Close, and when to use each one.
- Attitude: You will treat an ending as part of the service, not as an escape from the conversation.
- Skill: You will be able to end a contact with a how or what question, and keep a promise with a short update even when there is no news.
- Upskill: You will run the full arc of a contact with a friend or an AI partner — open, discover, steady, resolve, close — and record it, then score your own ending.

## Call Center Essentials
Slug: call-center-essentials

### Day 1: How a Call Center Works: Queues, Metrics, and the Quality Scorecard

- Knowledge: You will know what an inbound queue is, what causes the burst-and-lull pattern of a shift, what the three main channels are, and what each key metric measures in plain words.
- Attitude: You will treat metrics as a picture of behavior you control, not as a scoreboard that judges your worth.
- Skill: You will be able to explain, in your own words, how average handle time, first contact resolution, customer satisfaction, quality score, wrap-up time and adherence pull against each other.
- Upskill: You will be able to read a fictional queue where one metric was pushed hard and spot the cost that showed up in another metric.

### Day 2: The Call from Start to Finish: Opening, Verifying, Discovering, Hold, Transfer, Close

- Knowledge: You will know the four behaviors of the First-Contact Standard and the stages of a call: opening, verifying, discovering, hold, transfer and close.
- Attitude: You will treat the first few seconds with the same care you give to the resolution, because a caller decides whether to cooperate before you solve anything.
- Skill: You will be able to open a call calmly, verify identity, find the real issue behind the first complaint, place a caller on hold or transfer them with context, and close with a clear next step.
- Upskill: You will record and score your own role-play call against all four First-Contact behaviors, then repeat it and compare the two.

### Day 3: Voice and Words: Tone, Plain Language, Listening, and Clear Writing

- Knowledge: You will know how pace, intonation and volume carry confidence or doubt to a caller who cannot see you, and why plain, positive language keeps a caller listening.
- Attitude: You will treat your voice and your wording as the main tools of the job, not as decoration on top of the "real" work.
- Skill: You will be able to summarize a caller's issue back to them in their own terms before you solve it, and rewrite any phone answer as a short, plain email or chat message.
- Upskill: You will take one call transcript and deliver the same content two ways, a 60-second spoken summary and a four-line written message, and judge which one carried the tone better.

### Day 4: Upset Callers and Hard Moments: Pause, A.R.T., Saying No, Escalation

- Knowledge: You will know why an upset caller reacts before they reason, and how the Mandatory Pause and A.R.T. work together.
- Attitude: You will treat a pause and an early, clean escalation as professional acts, not signs of weakness.
- Skill: You will be able to run the full A.R.T. chain on an upset caller, say no to a request outside policy with a real alternative, and escalate early when the call is beyond your authority.
- Upskill: You will be able to keep calm and stay competent under repeated pushback, holding the pause before every reply.

### Day 5: Ready for the Job: Notes, Knowledge Base, Stress, and Your First 90 Days

- Knowledge: You will know what a complete call note contains, how to search a knowledge base fast, and how a 90-day plan protects a new agent from burnout.
- Attitude: You will treat notes and knowledge base searches as the customer's memory, not as paperwork, and treat stress recovery as part of the job rather than a weakness.
- Skill: You will be able to write a note the next agent can act on without calling you, search a knowledge base by the customer's own words and escalate a gap, and apply one behavior from quality feedback for a week.
- Upskill: You will be able to structure a role-play answer out loud in an interview or assessment setting, covering opening, verification, the real issue, a calm response to an upset caller and a confirmed next step.

# Track: Leadership

## Team Leader Essentials
Slug: team-leader-essentials

### Day 1: From Top Performer to Leader: What Changes

- MEDJAY's KASU structure sets out four objectives for this unit.
- Knowledge: You will know what changes when you move from individual contributor to team leader, and what research on new leaders says about how that shift usually goes.
- Attitude: You will accept that your success is now measured by the team's results, not by your own results.
- Skill: You will be able to delegate one task with a clear briefing, and set standards that are specific, equal for everyone and checkable.
- Upskill: You will hold an expectations conversation with a team member this week, including the one you are most tempted to avoid.

### Day 2: Setting Expectations and Running a Daily Huddle

- Knowledge: You will know the SBI (Situation, Behavior, Impact) model and the five parts of a focused daily huddle.
- Attitude: You will accept that an unclear expectation, not a lazy team member, is the most common reason performance drifts. Your job is to remove the fog, not to push harder.
- Skill: You will be able to write a clear expectation with a measure of success, run a 10-minute huddle, and deliver feedback that is specific, factual and easy to act on.
- Upskill: You will be able to turn a recurring blocker from your huddles into a change in how the team works, not just a problem you keep putting out.

### Day 3: Coaching and Feedback That Changes Behavior

- Knowledge: You will know The Question Ladder and its 4 rungs, and why the Impact rung is the one most leaders skip.
- Attitude: You will shift from being the person with the answers to being the person with the questions, and accept that a team member's own solution sticks better than yours.
- Skill: You will be able to run a short coaching conversation using The Question Ladder, and give feedback that describes specific behavior and its impact rather than the person's character.
- Upskill: You will record a five-minute coaching conversation, review it against The Question Ladder, and identify exactly where you slipped into telling instead of asking.

### Day 4: Handling Underperformance and Conflict

- Knowledge: Know why conflict handled early costs less than conflict ignored, why people separate themselves from the problem, and how to open a difficult conversation with A.R.T.
- Attitude: Treat raising a problem as a service to the team, not as disloyalty or drama. Decide that your silence is a decision.
- Skill: Run a structured underperformance conversation, mediate a clash between two team members using A.R.T., and turn a fight about people into an agreement about rules.
- Upskill: Lead one real underperformance or conflict conversation end to end this week, from the private first talk to the follow-up check, and log what you agreed.

### Day 5: Motivating, Recognizing, and Building Trust

- Knowledge: You will know what research says about specific goals, including their strengths and their risks, and why different team members are moved by different things.
- Attitude: You will treat recognition as a planned leadership behavior, not a reward you hand out when you happen to notice something, and accept that trust is built mainly by your consistency and fairness.
- Skill: You will be able to design a recognition approach for each team member and run a short conversation that connects their work to something that matters to them.
- Upskill: You will write your own personal leadership development goal in the four-part KASU structure, so you can use it again for future goals long after this course ends.

## Assistant Manager Essentials
Slug: assistant-manager-essentials

### Day 1: The Role and the Decision Map

- Knowledge: You will know the daily difference between the Team Leader, Assistant Manager and Manager roles, and the three options for any decision: own it, escalate it, or document it.
- Attitude: You will treat escalation as a strength and a sign of judgment, not a failure or a confession of weakness.
- Skill: You will be able to sort real decisions from your week into inside your authority, needs escalation, and needs a record, and escalate early with a clear recommendation.
- Upskill: You will hold a boundary-setting conversation with your manager that agrees, in plain words, what you decide alone, what you bring to them, and how fast you bring it.

### Day 2: Running the Day

- Knowledge: You will know the difference between urgent and important work, the purpose of daily planning blocks and opening and closing routines, the four parts of a short written brief, and what a follow-up rhythm agreed at handover looks like.
- Attitude: You will treat planning, briefing and a short daily huddle as core parts of the role, not as paperwork that gets in the way of real work.
- Skill: You will be able to sort your day's work by urgency and importance, write a short brief with outcome, steps, deadline and checkpoints, and agree a follow-up rhythm with a colleague at handover.
- Upskill: You will be able to run a daily huddle that ends with clear ownership and a deadline on every item, while keeping it short.

### Day 3: Leading People

- Knowledge: You will know the four parts of a clear expectation, what a short one-to-one check-in is for, and the three steps of SBI and A.R.T.
- Attitude: You will accept that most missed commitments are an expectation problem before they are a people problem, and that holding a boundary is a kindness to the whole team.
- Skill: You will be able to set an expectation in under a minute, run a fifteen-minute one-to-one that actually listens, give feedback on a missed commitment with SBI (Situation, Behavior, Impact), and hold a boundary with A.R.T.
- Upskill: You will hold that boundary when the person pushes back twice, without escalating the tone and without giving away the standard.

### Day 4: Problems and Decisions

- Knowledge: You will know why problems grow when the facts and assumptions are mixed together, and how to separate them.
- Attitude: You will see escalation as a strength when it comes with a recommendation, and see guessing as the real risk.
- Skill: You will be able to state a problem in one sentence, weigh two or three options by urgency and risk, make the routine decision, and explain it briefly.
- Upskill: You will be able to act as manager while your manager is away, including handling a customer escalation calmly and within your authority.

### Day 5: Reliability and Growth

- Knowledge: You will know how to keep a single commitments list, write a short weekly update to your manager, keep records someone else can pick up, and use A.R.T. when you receive feedback you do not fully agree with.
- Attitude: You will treat reliability as a behavior you can see and measure, not a personality trait you are born with, and treat escalation as a strength.
- Skill: You will be able to hold your routines steady under pressure, and to answer feedback you disagree with before you decide what to do with it.
- Upskill: You will be able to run a promotion conversation on your record rather than adjectives, and plan your first 90 days as three short phases.

## Store Manager Essentials
Slug: store-manager-essentials

### Day 1: Own the Whole Store

- Knowledge: You will know the difference between accountability and influence, and how to turn head office objectives into a few priorities, each with a measurable goal, a named owner and a check date.
- Attitude: You will accept that you own the store's result, including the parts other people carry out, and that protecting management time is part of the job, not a luxury.
- Skill: You will be able to delegate a task while keeping the accountability, and write a priority in one line that someone else could check without asking you what you meant.
- Upskill: You will apply both skills to a longer forward plan or to an interview answer, using your own situation, and explain out loud why managers drift back to the shop floor and what you will do about it.
- These four objectives come from KASU, MEDJAY's structure for every day's objectives: Knowledge, Attitude, Skill and Upskill. There is one of each, and each one is labeled.

### Day 2: Run the Store Day to Day

- Knowledge: You will know the steps of the customer journey from walk-in to repeat visit, and why each step needs one named owner.
- Attitude: You will treat the daily operation as something you design, not something that happens to you. You will stop patching the day and start shaping it.
- Skill: You will be able to match staff hours to footfall peaks, write standards that hold on a bad day, and find the store's bottleneck before adding staff.
- Upskill: You will write a short disruption plan for your store and use it to answer an interview question on how you run a busy day.

### Day 3: Build and Coach the Team

- Knowledge: You will know how to write an expectation as one observable behavior, how to give each area one owner, and how to give feedback with SBI (Situation, Behavior, Impact), which has three parts: the specific Situation, the observable Behavior, and the Impact.
- Attitude: You will see coaching as something that happens in short, frequent loops, not as an annual event or a difficult conversation you save up.
- Skill: You will be able to run a short coaching loop: name one behavior, give SBI feedback, practice the behavior right away, and agree a check date.
- Upskill: You will be able to coach a strong performer, address underperformance early and fairly, and settle friction between two team members without taking sides.

### Day 4: Read the Store's Numbers

- Knowledge: You will know the small balanced set of store numbers, what each one measures, and how to compare target with actual.
- Attitude: You will treat numbers as a question, not a verdict: a starting point for diagnosis, not a score to defend.
- Skill: You will be able to separate noise from trend, symptom from root cause, and rank a short list of actions by impact and effort, each with an owner, a measure and a review date.
- Upskill: You will apply the same reading to an underperforming area, such as one department, one shift or one product group, and explain your reasoning to someone who does not know the numbers.

### Day 5: Sustain Results and Plan 30 Days

- Knowledge: You will know the three plain steps for communicating up to your manager: report the facts, escalate what you cannot solve, recommend a way forward.
- Attitude: You will accept that telling your manager about a risk early, with a recommendation, protects the store rather than making you look weak.
- Skill: You will be able to build a one-page management review and a 30-day plan with no more than three priorities, each with a measure, an owner and a check date.
- Upskill: You will be able to adapt the same one-page review and 30-day plan to an interview answer for a store manager role.

## Area Manager Essentials
Slug: area-manager-essentials

### Day 1: Lead Sales Results Through Unit Managers

- Knowledge: You will know what an area manager is accountable for, where that ends, and what stays with unit managers.
- Attitude: You will shift from being the best salesperson in the unit to being the person who makes every unit manager better at running sales.
- Skill: You will build an area priority and risk map that names the priority, the risk, and the manager who owns each one.
- Upskill: You will set support boundaries for one real manager in your area, and explain to them what you will do and what you will not do.

### Day 2: Compare Results Without Misreading Performance

- Knowledge: You will know which sales indicators can be compared across units, which ones mislead when compared directly, and why conversion alone never identifies a cause.
- Attitude: You will treat every surprising number as a question, not a verdict, and hold performance conversations with that patience.
- Skill: You will calculate conversion and follow-up rates, spot high and low outliers, and test each interpretation against conversation evidence.
- Upskill: You will assemble a multi-unit comparison your managers can act on, and state one specific piece of conversation evidence that would confirm or overturn each reading.

### Day 3: Coach Managers to Improve Sales Conversations

- Knowledge: You will know how to separate observed selling behavior from assumptions during a performance review with a unit manager, and how to use SBI (Situation, Behavior, Impact) to give feedback on the manager's coaching.
- Attitude: You will treat your unit managers as developers of people, not as reps you can correct yourself. When a rep struggles, your instinct shifts from "I will fix this" to "I will teach the manager to fix this."
- Skill: You will be able to observe one coaching conversation, give specific feedback on the coaching behavior, agree a practice task, and set a measurable checkpoint.
- Upskill: You will run a full coaching plan for a struggling manager's team independently: a rewritten discovery question, a value explanation, and a follow-up checkpoint tied to a number.

### Day 4: Diagnose Shared Problems and Target Support

- Knowledge: You will know how to distinguish a local selling-execution gap from a recurring, area-wide obstacle, and which types of response fit each one.
- Attitude: You will treat a repeated selling problem as a signal about the system, not only about the person, and you will value targeted support over uniform pressure.
- Skill: You will be able to read lost-sale notes and customer conversations across several units, propose causes tied to evidence, choose between local coaching, shared product support, and a coordinated materials fix, and write a support allocation plan with owners, a limited pilot, and success measures.
- Upskill: You will be able to transfer an effective selling practice from one unit to others without forcing identical scripts, and you will know when a decision needs approval beyond your authority.

### Day 5: Run Area Reviews That Sustain Improvement

- Knowledge: what a concise area sales review contains, what belongs in an upward summary versus a unit manager's execution plan, and why unexplained silence reads to clients and senior leaders as uncertainty.
- Attitude: you run reviews to sustain improvement, not to perform activity. You report what is true, you recommend what you would do, and you leave execution with the manager who owns it.
- Skill: produce an area review summary and a 30-day improvement plan with measures, owners, support requests, and review dates, in a form a senior leader can act on in minutes.
- Upskill: lead a review conversation in which a senior leader makes a real decision and your unit managers leave knowing exactly what they own and when they will report back.

## Sales Manager Essentials
Slug: sales-manager-essentials

### Day 1: Sales Strategy, Priorities, and Business Alignment

- This course uses KASU, MEDJAY's framework for structuring objectives, developed by Steven Saad. It has 4 steps: Knowledge, Attitude, Skill and Upskill. Here are yours for today.
- Knowledge: You will know what makes a sales priority a real priority, and how to link each one to a business objective, a segment or channel, and the resources it will need.
- Attitude: You will accept that choosing priorities means choosing what not to pursue, and that a short list you can defend beats a long list that spreads effort evenly.
- Skill: You will be able to write a one-page sales priority plan that links each priority to a business objective, names the segment or channel it serves, and states what will deliberately not be pursued this cycle.
- Upskill: You will be able to present that plan to the people who will deliver it and defend the trade-offs with evidence and capacity, not opinion.

### Day 2: Target Planning and Resource Allocation

- Knowledge: You will know how to set a sales target from historical evidence, market context and team capacity, and how to split it across teams, territories, channels or segments with the assumptions written down.
- Attitude: You will treat a target as a documented decision rather than a figure handed down, and accept that defending your assumptions openly is part of the job.
- Skill: You will be able to allocate a target across several channels, name the owner of each slice, and set review points where the allocation is re-examined.
- Upskill: You will be able to present an allocation to a senior stakeholder and defend every assumption without retreating to ambition or optimism.

### Day 3: Sales KPIs, Forecasting, and Performance Analysis

- Knowledge: You will know the difference between activity metrics and outcome metrics, and how to build a forecast with visible assumptions.
- Attitude: You will treat numbers as evidence of behavior to be tested, not as verdicts to be accepted.
- Skill: You will be able to compare actuals against targets, spot a performance gap, and test whether a number is telling the truth before you act on it.
- Upskill: You will produce a short forecast for your own team with stated assumptions, and name the three performance risks that matter most.

### Day 4: Coaching, Accountability, and Performance Improvement

- Knowledge: You will know how to tell the difference between a skill gap, a process gap, a capacity problem and a misread expectation — and why each one needs a different corrective action.
- Attitude: You will stop treating coaching as something you do when there is time, and start treating it as the core of your role.
- Skill: You will be able to run an evidence-based coaching conversation using SBI feedback, agree a measurable commitment, and set a follow-up date.
- Upskill: You will write a complete performance improvement plan for a team or a manager, with actions, owners, measures and review dates.

### Day 5: Sales Reviews, Reporting, and Sustainable Growth

- Knowledge: You will know the difference between a review meeting that produces decisions and one that produces recaps, and you will know the four things every honest forecast report must contain.
- Attitude: You will treat the sales review as a decision forum, not a performance report card, and treat the forecast as your word to leadership, not a hopeful target in disguise.
- Skill: You will be able to run a sales review meeting that ends with tracked commitments, and prepare a concise performance review that states the number, the confidence, the assumptions and what would change the outcome.
- Upskill: You will lead a 30-day improvement plan from diagnosis to closure, coordinating with adjacent functions and evaluating whether the actions actually worked.
